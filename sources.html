    Sources

    This page lists what the database is built on. How the figures are used — which thresholds apply, which portion they are measured against, and what happens when two sources disagree — is in The method in detail.

    Nutrition figures

    Fat, protein, carbohydrate, fibre, sugar, water and alcohol per 100 g. Four national food composition tables, in this order. The order is about which shelf the food sits on, not about laboratory quality — all four are analysed data.

    1. Livsmedelsverket Sweden's national food composition database. First choice, because the site describes foods on a Swedish shelf.
    2. Frida Denmark's national table, DTU National Food Institute. Closest to Swedish conditions when Livsmedelsverket has no entry.
    3. Ciqual France's national table, ANSES. Answers for much of what is European but not Nordic — cheeses, charcuterie, pastries.
    4. USDA SR Legacy The United States reference set. Last resort, used mostly for spices and raw commodities where provenance matters least.

    Lactose and polyols are the exception to one source per food: Livsmedelsverket publishes neither, for any food, so those two columns are taken from Frida or Ciqual even when the rest comes from Livsmedelsverket. Nothing else is taken that way.

    FODMAPs

    • Monash University — which FODMAP types a food carries, and the largest serving that still rates low.
    • Monash rates servings and publishes no bulk export, so those figures are entered by hand, one food at a time.
    • Serving sizes are revised as foods are re-tested. Treat them as provisional until they have been checked against the current app.

    Allergens

    • EU Regulation 1169/2011, Annex II — the fourteen allergens that must be declared. The list is legal, not editorial: nothing is added and nothing is removed.
    • Onion and garlic and mushroom are tracked beyond the fourteen, precisely because no label has to mention them.

    The remaining properties

    Histamine, DAO competitors, salicylates, pollen cross-reactivity, alpha-gal and the GI irritant mechanisms have no gram threshold and come from clinical literature rather than from a table. How well established each one is appears in its own article, and is summarised in How certain is any of this?
