    Where the numbers come from

    Livsmedelsverket's food database — the Swedish Food Agency's national food composition database, roughly 2 600 foods with over 50 nutrients each — is the main reference for fat, protein, fiber, sugar and alcohol content in this tool. Every food below that is marked as checked has had its threshold-based traits compared against that database entry by entry.

    Not every food is in it. Cheeses like Roquefort and Fontina, spice blends like za'atar, and most Asian sauces have no Swedish entry, so their figures come from published nutrition data and clinical literature instead. Those foods are listed here too, marked as such — the point of this page is that you can tell which is which.

    Each food also shows the typical portion it is assessed on. A figure per 100g means little on its own — 17.5g of fat per 100g is about 4g in a slice of cheese and about 22g in a portion of mince — so the portion decides which threshold applies. Foods with a portion of 6g or less, like spices, are left out of the fat, protein and fiber thresholds altogether. See How the analysis works for the four portion bands.

    FODMAP subtypes come from the Monash University database rather than Livsmedelsverket, and allergens, cross-reactivity and GI irritant mechanisms come from clinical literature. Those are not what this page tracks. See How the analysis works for the full picture, and How Certain Is Any of This? for where the uncertainty sits.

    The meal builder also uses low-FODMAP serving sizes from the Monash University FODMAP Diet app — the largest serving of a food that still rates low. It uses them as a threshold rather than a quantity: a food weighed out within its serving is not counted as loading the meal. Monash rates servings and publishes no bulk export, so those figures are entered by hand, a food at a time, and only some of the FODMAP foods here have one yet. Treat them as provisional until they have been checked against the current app: serving sizes are revised as foods are re-tested. A meal always names the foods the threshold could not be applied to.
