What to do next

In order. Each source fills the gaps the one above it leaves, so running them out of order means confirming a match by hand that a better source would have covered.

Who does what

You never touch the repo. Everything below happens on this device, and the files come back to Claude to be committed.

You, on the phone

  1. Open this page. It says what is outstanding and which tool is next.
  2. Run that tool. Pick a match for each food — the score and the figures are both on screen, and the top line is often the wrong form of the right food.
  3. Download the two files it offers: the aliases (what you confirmed) and the generated figures.
  4. Open build-nutrition.html. Step 2 takes the Livsmedelsverket export; step 3 takes the generated file you just downloaded. Download nutrition-data.js.
  5. Send Claude all three, and say which round they came from.

Claude, in the repo

  • Commits the three files, and adds a new nutrition-*.js to ASSETS in sw.js the first time one appears — otherwise check-site calls it a stray root file.
  • Runs check-data.js and check-site.js, bumps the version, pushes. Those two never run on a phone.
  • Owns anything the pages cannot reach: foods-data.js — new foods, traits, portions — the doses, the ladder, and which derivation codes USDA figures are accepted on.

One commit is needed between sources. Each audit offers the foods with no figures as of the nutrition-data.js in the repo, and these pages read it from there. So finish Denmark, send it over, wait for it to land — then start the American round, or it will offer you foods Denmark has already covered. check-data.js fails on that pair rather than leaving it to be noticed.

Reloading a tool page after a commit is enough to pick up the change: nothing under /tools/ is ever cached. Your picks live in this browser and are merged with whatever is in the repo, so a half-finished round survives both a reload and a commit.

The ladder

Which source wins where two of them have a figure for the same food. Set in tools/nutrition-core.js and loadFallbacks in both builders — not here.

    Where the source files come from

    None of them are kept in the repo — they are large and they are somebody else's. Download fresh each round.

    • Livsmedelsverket — Sök näringsinnehåll, Ladda ner Livsmedelsdatabasen. Excel, CSV or JSON all work.
    • Frida — frida.fooddata.dk, the full dataset as Excel. Built against version 6.1. Names every food in English as well as Danish, so there is no translation step.
    • USDA — fdc.nal.usda.gov, as JSON. SR Legacy (64MB, ~7,800 foods — the one worth having) and Foundation Foods (7MB, 363 foods, mostly raw produce Sweden already covers).

    Decisions already made

    Settled, with the reasoning in method.html. Listed here so they are not reopened from scratch.

    • Two sets are refused outright. USDA Branded Foods is manufacturer label data rounded to legal tolerances. FNDDS is survey data costed out of recipes rather than measured. Neither is read.
    • USDA figures are taken one at a time, not by food. Every figure records how it was arrived at. Taken: measured, calculated by definition (carbohydrate by difference, protein from nitrogen), and the table's own "not naturally present". Dropped: manufacturer-supplied, label-derived, recipe- or formulation-estimated, copied from another nutrient, and carried over from another form of the same food. An unrecognised code is dropped and reported in USDA's own words — decide it in ACCEPTED or REJECTED in usda-core.js, once. Two are still undecided: T and JA.
    • No fabricated zeros. A food with no figure gets none, and the meal builder refuses it rather than counting it as zero. A USDA entry may therefore carry fewer fields than a Swedish one, and the gaps are deliberate.
    • Fiber is not comparable across methods. AOAC 2011.25 reads higher than 991.43 on all 17 foods that carry both, median +1.8g/100g — 30% of the 6.1g dose — and the gap falls on starch, because resistant starch is what the newer method counts. We take 991.43 first to match Sweden and Denmark; each entry records which was used.
    • Generated files are never hand-edited. nutrition-data.js, nutrition-frida.js and nutrition-usda.js all come out of a tool. Edit the alias file and re-run instead.

    Traps that have actually bitten

    Each of these cost real time once. They are all guarded now — this is so the guard is not mistaken for noise.

    • A generated file smaller than its alias file. A re-run once wrote only the newly confirmed matches: 30 confirmed came out as a 2-food file, and rebuilding would have stripped the other 28. Both this page and the build page warn on it now.
    • Running the audits out of order. Each one offers the foods with no figures as of the repo's nutrition-data.js, so the first one run claims them. check-data.js fails on a food holding two matches.
    • The top candidate is usually the wrong form. Every bad match this project has had was fresh-for-dried or raw-for-cooked. That is what the figures under each candidate are for: a dried food with 80g of water is not the dried one.
    • Stale files served by the service worker. Anything at the root goes through it even when the page asking sits under /tools/. Every tool fetches with cache: "no-store" for that reason; keep it that way.
    • Foods sold dry and eaten made up. Soup powder, drink mix. The table lists the powder, the portion is the bowl. A madeUp recipe on the food scales it — do not type diluted figures in by hand.
    • A plausible explanation is not the cause. A caching story once explained a wrong figure perfectly and was wrong; the real fault was a field being dropped before either tool saw it. The checks named it three different ways without being told what to look for. Run them before believing anything.

      Where the long version lives

      • tools/worklist.md — every decision and every fault, in the order they happened, with the numbers. The place to look when something here reads as arbitrary.
      • method.html — the published reasoning: doses, portions, sources, what the fiber methods do to the column.
      • The header comment in each core file — lmv-core.js, frida-core.js, usda-core.js each open with what is different about that source and why the file exists.

      Nothing important lives only in a conversation. If it is not in one of those three, it is not written down.
