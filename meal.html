What this tool does

  • It shows what a set of foods have in common.
  • It lists which foods in its database carry a given trait.

What it does not do

  • It does not diagnose anything.
  • It does not tell you what to eat or what to avoid. No list here is a diet.
  • A food carrying a trait does not mean you react to it. Most people react to none of them.
  • A food not carrying a trait does not mean it is safe for you.

Before you change what you eat

  • Talk to a doctor or dietitian first. Cutting foods out on your own can hide a real diagnosis, and long avoidance can leave your diet short of things you need.
  • This is a tool for health care professionals, and it is NOT meant to replace their diagnosis and advice.

Build a meal and see what it is loaded with.

  1. Add foods with their weights. Type a food, give it a weight in grams, add it to the meal. Only foods with nutrient figures on file can go in — a meal is reported in grams, and a food with no numbers would look like it counted.
  2. Add more meals if you want to compare. A day, a week, or the two meals that behaved differently.
  3. Read what the meal carries. Amount-based traits are summed; the rest are listed as present.

Nothing here is saved. Use Save to file to keep a copy on your own device, and Load from file to bring it back.
